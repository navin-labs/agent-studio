# Faceless motion-designed Reels: what holds and grows (Forge, 2026-10-25)

Source: Forge's read-only Instagram study, 45 Reels (15 business automation, 15 history and inventions, 10 cross-niche, 5 low performers), posted 2026-05-03 to 2026-10-23. Forge kept the per-Reel JSON (`reels.json`) and raw reads.

## How much to trust it
- Ranked by **likes / followers**: native access shows no views, saves, shares or retention. Small accounts are noisy (4 likes on 8 followers is 0.50); large accounts post low ratios with huge absolute likes.
- **Not measured** for any Reel: transcripts, first spoken lines, words per second, pauses, cuts per 10 s, still moments, payoff timing, music tempo, exact colours. Durations exist for 4 Reels only (12 to 30 s).
- What is solid: hook *type* and opening image, structure, which devices appear, word-to-picture match, CTA kind, caption first line, comment rates.

## Findings (Top 15 by ratio vs Low 5)
| Pattern | Top 15 | Low 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Payoff device (a closing result or image) | 14 | 3 |
| Novelty / open loop | 13 | 3 |
| Reveal (the mechanism or answer shown) | 12 | 2 |
| Literal word-to-picture match | 11 | 2 |
| Hook is a concrete **scene** (an opening image, not a talking point) | 10 | 1 |
| Single story structure | 6 | 0 |
| Countdown / list structure | 0 | 2 |

1. **Open on one concrete scene** (a man rolling a wheel alone, burrs on a dog, chocolate melting in a pocket) or one moving number (a fee counter falling). Never on a dashboard, a logo or a list title.
2. **Story order that wins:** accident or struggle, a failed attempt, the mechanism, dated proof, a payoff **image**. The top Reel (28.85) is a failure loop: the wheel alone fails, the first cart collapses, the spoked cart works, ending on the cart riding into the sunset.
3. **End on a picture, not a summary line**: the cart at sunset, the pin in a glass case, the modern uses montage.
4. **Never a countdown or list** ("7 accidental inventions"): it dilutes one surprising image into seven thin ones.
5. **Archival photos at the end are the trust device** for animated history (ravak.media Velcro, 7.22): the animation tells, the real photos prove.
6. **Comment-keyword CTA** tied to a concrete free thing (a file, a link, a system) is the only CTA with extreme comment rates (comments over likes: FOLDER 10,953 vs 4,507; AI 4,610 vs 2,653). Bare "follow for more" does not separate winners.
7. **Caption first line**: a short claim or question ("I FIRED MYSELF. FROM ONE TASK."), never a channel trailer ("Follow for more...").
8. **Repetition**: winners reuse one visual grammar per account and change the story, problem and hook image every time. The same format scored 7.22 with the more surprising hook (burrs) and 1.70 with the less surprising one (melted chocolate): the hook sets the ceiling.
9. **Narrate** every story and explainer; music-only works for showreels only.
10. Non-English origin stories took the top 3 ratios (Hindi 28.85, Persian caption 7.22, Telugu 3.19).

## Formats seen that we do not have (build candidates)
- **3D mechanism explainer** (build, fail, fix): a cutaway model with labelled parts, flow arrows, a design flaw shown, then the fix, ending on a second-person "you invented it".
- **Object-narrator storybook**: the invention itself as a cute character, step-by-step making, a museum glass-case finale.
- **Slide-deck**: one bold statement per full-frame slide, one spot illustration, numbered steps (01 CAPTURE, 02 UNDERSTAND...).
- **UI-mockup demo**: mock app windows, a typed request, documents assembling from it.
- **Neon UI diagram**: dark grid, neon tool cards, a draining meter as the metaphor, arrow chains of steps.
- **Minimalist text explainer**: beige, dark text, one accent colour, three text-led steps, a sample-data card, a named human-check beat.
- **Kinetic type**: a word built in stages; generative glyph grids with colour cycling (cold opens).

## Key Takeaways
- Scene hook, failure-loop story, payoff image, archival proof at the end, no lists.
- Comment-keyword CTA and claim-style caption lines drive engagement.
- One grammar per series; a new story and hook image every time.
- Ratios are likes/followers and noisy; timing and voice data were not measurable, so voice pacing rules still come from our own measurements.
